# M09: Model selection

Owner: Member 3. Phase 3. Status: itinerary comparison executed; independent human review pending.

Course checkpoint: Compare choice plus two alternatives.
[Source](https://cs3216.github.io/coursework/artificial-intelligence#using-the-right-model-for-the-job).

## Answer

We provisionally retain **OpenAI GPT-4.1 mini** for itinerary generation because this stage needs structured text, reliable use of saved place IDs, fixed bookings and realistic times at interactive latency. In a current-prompt experiment covering 14 synthetic scenarios, it delivered **10/13 feasible plans**, compared with **7/13 for GPT-4o mini** and **5/13 for local Qwen3 4B through Ollama**. A stricter target/meal/suggestion/seasonal-text proxy passed 7/13, 6/13 and 0/13 respectively. The incumbent's all-run median was 6.27 seconds and estimated token cost $0.00194/request. Its ten accepted plans fit the observed production timing limits.

GPT-4o mini was roughly 59% cheaper per request but delivered three fewer feasible plans; it remains a credible cost challenger. Local Qwen avoids API token fees but lost saved-place identity in accepted outputs and needed 62 seconds for its accepted five-day response. Gemini 3.6 Flash was also tested, but API errors prevented a defensible quality ranking. We do not interpret those errors as inferior model intelligence. The deterministic baseline passed all 13 feasible cases but lacked richer suggestions and seasonal guidance, motivating a future hybrid design.

We used an 8,000-token ceiling, at most one validation-guided repair, and structured JSON output with the same compiler for every model. The comparison used temperature 0.2, leaving top-p and penalties at defaults. A separate balanced study tested temperature 0.2 versus 1.0: final acceptance was 7/8 versus 8/8, with medians 3.30 versus 4.64 seconds. This small sample does not prove an optimum or support lowering production temperature. Production still omits temperature and retains its 25-second call / 40-second total deadlines; the diagnostic benchmark allowed 90/150 seconds and reported timing-budget fit separately.

The capability matrix covers context, modalities, tools, cost and provider constraints. Actual completed inputs were fewer than 3,700 tokens, so million-token context was not a reason to prefer a model. Multimodal and tool support were documented, not experimentally evaluated at this text-only stage.
