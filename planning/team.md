# Four-person ownership

Names and strengths are pending. Assign real people to these slots at kickoff.
Assumption: comparable availability. Points estimate relative effort and include testing,
documentation, and review; they are not hours or a promise of identical difficulty.

| Slot | Product ownership | Graded milestone answers | Primary reviewer | Points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Member 1 | Traveler UI, inbox screens, map, magazine, landing page | M01, M04, M16, M17, M18 | Member 4 | 40 |
| Member 2 | AI extraction, place evidence, prompt/model evaluation | M02, M07, M08, M09, M11 | Member 3 | 40 |
| Member 3 | Preferences, schedule engine, edits, timing/cost decisions | M03, M06, M10, M12, M15 | Member 2 | 40 |
| Member 4 | Identity, database, jobs, private sharing, release, analytics | M05, M13, M14, M19, M20 | Member 1 | 40 |

Each member owns five graded answers, including one of the four lower-weight milestones.
M00 and final pitch are shared; Member 1 assembles M00 and Member 4 assembles the final package.
Review is reciprocal across the two pairs. Cross-boundary changes also involve the affected owner.

## Avoid bottlenecks

- Member 4 establishes identity/data/job interfaces first. Others build against fixtures immediately.
- Member 2 publishes candidate-place examples before completing extraction.
- Member 3 publishes itinerary/conflict examples before completing the scheduler.
- Member 1 builds the three views from the same itinerary contract.
- Each owner writes the evidence for their own code; Member 4 does not write everyone's report.
- Member 1 supplies visuals and UX findings; Member 2 supplies accuracy measurements;
  Member 3 supplies schedule and cost measurements; Member 4 supplies access and release evidence.

## Fairness checks

At each roadmap gate, compare remaining points, actual effort, blockers, and review load.
If someone has over 20% more remaining effort than the team average, transfer an unstarted task
and update its owner/reviewer. Split an unexpectedly large task before transferring it.
Preserve 20% of each person's available time for review, integration, and fixes.

Record actual hours if useful; never use commit counts or the number of documents as a contribution score.
All four people attend one test session, review another member's work, and contribute to the final pitch.
